## Synthesis

**Representation is only one coordinate.** Kolda and Bader establish the vocabulary for higher-order factorization; Oseledets formalizes the tensor-train representation; Cichocki and colleagues and Sidiropoulos and colleagues place tensor networks in a broader optimization and signal-processing context. These sources justify treating matrix, tensor train, Tucker, rank, and tensorization layout as explicit design choices. They do not imply that two decompositions with nominally equal rank have equal parameter counts or deployment bytes. We therefore compare actual final-model bytes and identify where structure is bundled with an execution layout.

**Prior compression research already varies structure, rank, and objectives.** Journal studies of coupled, hybrid, nonlinear tensor-train, tensor-ring, and Tucker compression show that a paper cannot claim tensor factorization itself as new. Bayesian rank selection, tensor-shape search, controllable matrix decomposition, pruning combinations, and quantization-aware factorization further make rank allocation, tensorization, repair, and mixed compression established directions. Their experimental domains and implementation paths vary. Our contribution is a controlled *conditional comparison* of legal configurations under a fixed language model and budget, not a new decomposition algorithm or a faithful implementation of each cited paper.

**Modern LLM-specific work narrows the novelty claim.** The journal survey of LLM compression frames pruning, quantization, distillation, and low-rank methods as separate families. Saten applies sparse-augmented tensor networks to post-training LLM compression; the Fisher-weighted decomposition study, SVD-LLM, and COALA establish task/context/activation-sensitive approximation ideas; the recent AWT preprint explicitly studies activation-derived preconditioning of tensorization. Accordingly, “activation-aware tensor compression” and “Fisher-aware low-rank compression” are not claimed as new. Some published configurations cannot be exactly reconstructed from public source evidence in this project; we label our implementations as local adaptations and preserve formula and code-path ambiguity rather than asserting author-code reproduction.

**Repair and deployment must be measured separately.** Distillation and parameter-efficient adaptation papers provide a rationale for separating construction-time objective from post-compression repair, while the neural-network coding standard and DeepCABAC remind us that mathematical parameter count is distinct from a serialized artifact. Hardware-oriented surveys likewise distinguish storage, latency, memory, and energy. Our study measures final payload bytes exactly; decoding time, allocated CUDA memory, board energy, and preparation time are reported on their actual available denominators. Historical and new cost batches are observational rather than isolated hardware interventions, so we make no causal speed or energy claim.

**What remains open in this paper's scope.** The tested hypothesis is that objective and repair condition the ordering of the *complete* structure/layout paths, and that objective conditions the semantic-versus-alternative Tucker layout ordering. Development evidence comes from the predeclared legal grids; selected directions are evaluated on new, non-overlapping source clusters without retraining the existing models. This confirms only the fixed-model, fixed-data-source contrasts. It does not establish cross-model transfer, independent training-seed variation, superiority over every current compression system, or external benchmark performance. The 12 of 36 Step5 cells with identical three-label NLL vectors reinforce why nominal seed labels are not treated as independent statistical units.
